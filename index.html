THE CHALLENGE

I DIDN'T INHERIT A CLEAN PROJECT.

I took over the implementation in February 2018 after the project was already underway. Requirements were still evolving, major deliverables needed clearer definition and multiple technical workstreams were competing for attention under significant schedule pressure.

My responsibility was to bring structure to the ambiguity: establish requirements, formalize change, coordinate technical teams and give stakeholders a clear path from business need to executable delivery.

TURNING AMBIGUITY

FEATURE SPOTLIGHT / QDA

MAKING QUALITY
VISIBLE.

The Quality Defect Application gave production personnel a graphical way to identify where a defect occurred on a manufactured component and record the appropriate defect classification.

Operators could identify the affected area directly on an image of the part, select the defect category and drive the corresponding quality disposition and traceability workflow.

CONTROLLED CHANGE

THE REQUIREMENTS
KEPT MOVING.

As manufacturing requirements evolved, the QDA application expanded beyond its original scope. New requirements included traceable labeling, automatic printing in Injection and Paint, support for defects spanning multiple areas of a component and flexible printer routing.

I authored and coordinated the formal change-order process used to define the new work, establish development effort, communicate project impact and move approved requirements into implementation.

06

SHOP-FLOOR VISIBILITY

DATA WHERE THE
WORK HAPPENS.

A separate Visualization workstream brought graphical operational information directly onto flat-panel displays throughout the manufacturing floor.

The solution included 26 display pages designed around production and quality information such as scrap-versus-good ratios, machine status, downtime and quality alerts.

07

DELIVERY

FROM DOCUMENTATION
TO THE FACTORY FLOOR.

Delivery required far more than documentation and status meetings. I spent significant time onsite working directly with developers, consultants, managers and frontline manufacturing personnel.

Other components, including the U9 integration, were introduced through subsequent phases rather than through a single all-at-once cutover.
